Extraneous cognitive load caused by how information is presented and organized. The classic case: a geometry diagram with the information you need to solve for its sides and angles printed separately, so you keep looking back and forth between the two.

The fix is boring and effective: put the labels on the diagram, integrate text and figure, make the eye travel less. Holding one source in mind while searching the other is pure overhead — working memory spent on the layout instead of the geometry ([Cognitive Load](/notes/cognitive-load)).

The other workaround is to move part of the information into a different channel — narrate the text while the eyes stay on the figure — which is the [Modality Effect](/notes/modality-effect). Hyperlinks are the same problem at web scale ([Effects of Hyperlinks on Learning](/notes/effects-of-hyperlinks-on-learning)), and so is any textbook that puts the figure on the next page.

An individual is a crowd, and a crowd is an individual. That's how talking with groups feels: the reversion to the mean. The conversation can't escape or change topic except to jump to the most identifiable idea, rather than the deepest thought or insight we could try to inspire in each other.

Part of it is arithmetic. In a group, every topic has to be accessible to everyone present, so the conversation drifts to the intersection of interests, and the intersection is small. Part of it is social risk: a strange idea said to one person is a conversation; said to six, it's a performance.

Depth needs a small audience and some tolerance for silence, which is why one-on-one goes further — and why [Shared presence is enough for friendship](/notes/shared-presence-is-enough-for-friendship): the group's job may be presence rather than insight. The shared reference points a group falls back on are [Collective myths](/notes/collective-myths) in miniature, and [People pay far less attention to you than you think](/notes/people-pay-far-less-attention-to-you-than-you-think) is the reminder that the performance risk is smaller than it feels.

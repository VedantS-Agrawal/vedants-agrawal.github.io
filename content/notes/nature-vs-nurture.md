Which contributes more to how an organism turns out — genes or environment?

[Francis Galton](/notes/francis-galton) decided nature "prevails enormously" over nurture, at least for extraordinary traits. Later discoveries disproved the strong version, but he was the one who backed a claim about heredity of behavior with research.

What I took from <span class="link-private" title="Not public yet">Nature Via Nurture</span>:
- **The paradox of heritability.** The more we equalize opportunity in a society, the more the remaining variation in performance comes down to genes.
- **Genes as agents of nurture.** As people age, less of their intelligence correlates with shared environment and more with genes. Two kids in one home may perform alike early; once they choose their own environments, their genes steer them apart.
- Nurture can be as irreversible as nature — critical periods in vision and language show it.

To forcibly exist on either side of this argument would be a failure to understand the relationship. There's flexibility within the confines of the structure nature gave me. Part of my person is flexible and part isn't — part is amenable to argument, and part is enforcing behaviors passed down through inheritance.

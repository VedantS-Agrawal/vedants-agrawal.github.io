Humans are always going to complain — it's in our nature to acclimate to whatever condition we're in. So the complaints we have about our environment reflect how well our needs are already met.

From Maslow's <span class="link-private" title="Not public yet">The Farther Reaches of Human Nature</span>: low grumbles are about food, safety, belonging. Metagrumbles are about knowledge, beauty, creativity — metaneeds that only become available to complain about once the lower ones are handled. Someone complaining that the rose gardens in the park aren't cared for well enough is, in its way, a wonderful thing: it shows the height of life they're living at.

What a way to read a person's development — by the quality of their grumbling. The question becomes what they're complaining about: their ability to be actualized? The kinds of problems they're working on?

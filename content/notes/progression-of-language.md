The move from pictorial to phonetic alphabets changed our mental capacities.

Sumerian cuneiform and Egyptian hieroglyphs were logosyllabic: the reader first had to analyze a character to work out how it was being used. That took more processing, and made literacy slow to learn and slow to spread.

The Greeks' phonetic alphabet created an *economy of characters* — less time and attention to recognize symbols, fewer cognitive resources. Brain studies comparing readers of phonetic and pictorial scripts back this up.

The cost of abstraction: McLuhan thought preliterate people had a more "sensuous" involvement with the world. Literacy and the analytical thinking it brings let us abstract away from lived experience — and detach from feeling.

Learning Spanish: it's hard to transfer how I think into the language. Thoughts flow differently, bounded by the words I know. I think less politically and less complexly, because the words don't come quickly. In English I can convey thoughts with subtlety — or, put another way, be wordy. In Spanish there's no room for long, drawn-out sentences; I have to finish forming the thought first.

The other direction is true too: people who know different languages live a different experience of the world. Different words for facets of nature and environment make reality richer and more detailed.

And words have a cost. They might reduce our capacity to experience the world directly — pulling us outside the body, sorting things into categories. What I can experience in any state of mind isn't fully expressible in words; it's the difference between labeling an experience and not labeling it.

On learning vocabulary: mapping a Spanish word back to English is three layers of processing (thing → English → Spanish) instead of two. Better to tie the word directly to an image, idea, or felt sensation.

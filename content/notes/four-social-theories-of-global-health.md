From a medical anthropology class in Seville. Medical anthropology studies health, healing, and illness within their political, economic, and social context.

1. **Unintended consequences of social action.** Institutions trying to do good cause later harm. Forced vaccination campaigns bred vaccine hesitancy that undermined later public-health efforts.
2. **Social suffering.**
   - Health systems can do harm — medical error, waiting times, misdiagnosis, poorly informed treatment.
   - Social causes produce health problems — poverty, high-stress environments, food deserts, no sanitation or clean water, toxic exposure.
   - Social and health problems are hard to separate: is someone ill because of a disease, or the chronic stress of living in poverty? Social policy and health policy are often one and the same.
   - The individual isn't the only sufferer. A person with Alzheimer's has a family watching them lose their memory; disease ripples through social networks.
3. **Social construction of reality.** What health is, what counts as acceptable treatment and healthy behavior — all depend on the cultural reality people live in.
4. **Biopower.** Governments and corporations shape how our biology manifests — which products they offer, which choices they allow, which body types get manufactured consent.

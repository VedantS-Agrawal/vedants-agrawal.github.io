Technology has the capacity to drain meaning out of our work by separating understanding from mechanical, through our senses, to that of computerized control. Our own interpretive muscles are supplanted by computer intelligence. 

What is special about this way of interacting with the world -- it demands dialogue, clarification, objection, revision. One is dealing with the closer substrates of reality. When our way of interacting with the world becomes mediated through obfuscated black boxes of statistics and software, it loses meaning. Individual skill and the individual identity around it becomes sacrificed for advanced overall performance.

What happened to having to learn how to play music to listen ot it? With the advent of radio and record players, the parlor piano no longer demonstrated ones intimacy with music and it was replaced with a needle scrolling over the skill of another. We have inherited conceptual and physical tools that we can no longer make. We assemble results that we trust but cant personally reproduce. 

The distinction between information and skill has become very blurry in the era of large language models. They generate responses on the fly, dependent on a model with pretrained weights on the crystallized knowledge of the past. 

AI in medicine may reduce the accuracy of any one individual diagnostician, but the digital centaur approach still leads to benefits in diagnosis.

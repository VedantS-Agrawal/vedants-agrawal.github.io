Set up the morning to use its 3–4 hours of concentration, and let the rest of the day be carried by the wind and the remaining tasks.

Much better than cramming work that needs serious focus into short gaps where I can't focus deeply on it anyway. See [Deep Work](/library/deep-work) on attention residue.

It's scheduling by quality instead of quantity. An hour of real concentration does more than three hours of fragments, and fragments are what an unplanned afternoon tends to produce. Protecting the morning also takes a decision off the table — no weighing what to do first when the first thing is already decided ([Decision quality falls as decision count rises](/notes/decision-quality-falls-as-decision-count-rises)).

The rest of the day can then be shallow without guilt, because the important work already happened. The failure mode is spending those hours on email and calling it a head start, which is the warning in <span class="link-private" title="Not public yet">Either you run the day or the day runs you</span>. A walk between blocks is the cheapest reset on offer ([The Cognitive Benefits of Interacting with Nature](/library/the-cognitive-benefits-of-interacting-with-nature)).

The clock made "the abstract framework of divided time" the point of reference for thought and action — and with it, belief in a world of mathematically measurable sequences.

- For most of history, time was a continuous, cyclical flow, set by agrarian rhythms. Things just took as long as they took; there was no way to measure whether something was done "better."
- Personal timekeeping put every individual in control of their own time — possibly a stimulus to Western individualism.

This connects to *Four Thousand Weeks*. I agree that mechanizing time made room for productivity to be a topic at all. Once you can measure how long something takes, you can show objectively whether it's "efficient" — and the pressure to be efficient follows.

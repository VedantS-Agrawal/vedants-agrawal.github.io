Some problems are effectively intractable — they're solved in O(n!) time, so the truly optimal answer can't be reached in a tractable amount of time. Relax the constraints and you reach a semi-optimal solution in a fraction of the time.

Its cousin is overfitting: certain data values get over-prioritized, and the model stops generalizing beyond the data set it was built on.

Both carry over to human decisions. I can overfit a decision — too many variables, too many what-ifs. Relaxing the constraints cuts through it. The question that does this: *what job would you pick if you won the lottery?* It lets me look past the variables that distract from the intention behind the choice, and decide with less effort and less excessive weighing of every factor.

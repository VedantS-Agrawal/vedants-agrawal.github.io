Why it came up: the [Worked Example Effect](/notes/worked-example-effect) raised worries about passive learning, and learners tended to study worked examples only when they struggled with conventional problems.

The fix was paired alternation — study an example, then solve a problem — which recruits both passive and active learning. A **completion problem** goes further: a partially worked example where the learner completes the key steps.

Result: people learning programming wrote better new programs when their learning was built on completion problems rather than writing programs from scratch.

The voice in our head is constantly trying to find a comfortable resting place — *internal coherence*, the brain's need to fit new information into its existing schemas. It will change viewpoints and shift perspective to get there. It reminds me of the hedonic treadmill: this voice keeps moving the set-point for silence.

Is the voice me? No — I'm the one observing it. Put three objects in front of you and ask which one you are; none of them, you're the one looking. Same for the inner voice.

The commotion the mind makes about life is the real problem. Thinking about how things should change, or disliking how they are, does nothing to change them.

Why do we narrate? Labeling what we observe makes us feel we have a relationship with it — it's been seen, labeled, judged, run through our values and history. There's always some thought that makes the experience feel controlled: noticing it's cold, then comforting yourself that you'll be home in a few minutes. What about that thought was necessary?

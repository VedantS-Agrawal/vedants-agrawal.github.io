Biphasic sleep is our natural physiological state — before artificial light, humans tended toward two sleeps — and naps can be used to improve performance through the day.

What gets in the way:
- Self-medicating sleepiness with caffeine. Could time the caffeine right before a midday nap instead.
- A workaholic culture that looks down on napping. Worth checking, with <span class="link-private" title="Not public yet">Cognitive Journaling</span>, whether I hold beliefs that keep me from using naps.

What the evidence says:
- 10–15 minute naps improve cognitive performance. In some studies 20-minute naps improved reaction time and vigilance without changing subjective sleepiness; 30-minute naps improved both.
- A nap beat 200 mg of caffeine and lasted longer; nap plus caffeine beat either alone. Bright light and face-washing did worse than both.
- Sleep stage matters: stage 4 left people sleepier, REM less sleepy; stage 1 without stage 2 improved alertness.
- In a 4-day visual task, performance fell each session. A 30-minute nap after session two stopped the decline; a 1-hour nap reversed it.
- A Greek study: napping 30 minutes at least 3 times a week went with 37% less risk of dying from a heart attack (64% among working men).

The "perfect" nap is 90 minutes between 1 and 3 pm — its ratio of sleep stages mirrors a night's.

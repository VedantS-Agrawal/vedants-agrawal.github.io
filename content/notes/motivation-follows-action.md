I wait for motivation to exercise, and it doesn't come. I know the answer: motivation follows action. I'm just not taking any action.

Russell says something similar about the feeling that everything is vanity — those moods can't be changed by rational argument or words, only by the necessity of action. See <span class="link-private" title="Not public yet">The Conquest of Happiness</span>.

Russell's version is less about mood than about design: the human animal is built for some struggle, and "the mere absence of effort from his life removes an essential ingredient of happiness." *— Bertrand Russell, The Conquest of Happiness* Waiting for motivation is a way of removing the effort and expecting the happiness anyway.

The mechanism isn't mysterious. Starting produces small signals of progress, and progress is what motivation feeds on; waiting produces nothing. The trick is making the first step small enough to be beneath negotiation — shoes on, one set, one page. [Limbic friction](/notes/limbic-friction) names the resistance at the threshold; [When I feel purposeless or anxious, I should work out](/notes/when-i-feel-purposeless-or-anxious-i-should-work-out) is the applied version.

An idea I kept running into across my reading: collective myths bond us together and form the basis for our social hierarchies — what we believe in, pursue, look up to; what we take as good and bad, right and wrong, valuable and not. They shape how we think and try to understand the world.

They let us feel connected to and understanding of the people around us, and they give us the organizational structure and shared vision for larger projects.

Take the American Dream: rugged individualism, Puritan hard work, inalienable rights, the idea that a man should govern himself rather than be beholden to a larger body — voicing his opinion when larger projects need it, keeping his own sphere of influence with limited government.

From [Sapiens](/library/sapiens): humans top out at about 150 people for structures built on intimate bonds. Past that, cultural myths, laws, religion, gods — structures that exist only in other people's minds — coordinate effort toward shared goals. And <span class="link-private" title="Not public yet">The Search for Meaning</span>: we don't escape myth by sticking to "facts"; understanding anything means encoding it through mythic techniques.

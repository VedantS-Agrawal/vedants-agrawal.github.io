Every self-referential thought should go under the magnifying glass: is it contributing to action toward my goals?

What good does thinking I'm unintelligent do? It makes me shirk and stop taking in information, believing I can't understand it.

The test is functional, not moral. "I'm bad at this" isn't wrong because it's negative; it's wrong because it does no work — it doesn't change the next action except to make it less likely. "I don't understand this yet, so I need an example" is just as self-referential, and it earns its place.

The stakes are higher than mood: "We can become, neurologically, what we think." *— Nicholas Carr, The Shallows* Repeated thoughts are rehearsal. The audit is the same move cognitive therapy borrowed from the Stoics (<span class="link-private" title="Not public yet">Disturbed not by things but by our view of them</span>); the thing under audit is [The Voice Inside Your Head](/notes/the-voice-inside-your-head).

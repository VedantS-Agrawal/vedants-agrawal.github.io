One of the observations that led Darwin to natural selection: 14 finch species on the Galapagos whose main difference was the beak, each one suited to how that species fed.

They share a single common ancestor, so the differences really are adaptations. Field work has since watched selection happen in these populations: in years of heavy rain or drought, different food sources become common, and the species (and individuals) that can reach them thrive.

The same work shows how the birds keep species apart — visual cues (beak, appearance) and song — which prevents cross-species breeding that would otherwise homogenize the gene pool.

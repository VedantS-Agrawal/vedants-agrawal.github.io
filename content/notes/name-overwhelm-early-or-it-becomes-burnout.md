Being able to identify the feeling of being overwhelmed is crucial to managing workload. Failing to notice it is a recipe for burnout.

Overwhelm is a signal with a short shelf life. Named early, it's a scheduling problem: too many commitments, some of which can be dropped, delegated, or moved. Ignored, it compounds into exhaustion and cynicism, and by then the problem is no longer the calendar.

The naming is the hard part, because busyness feels like competence from the inside ([Productivity means intentional progress, not busyness](/notes/productivity-means-intentional-progress-not-busyness)). A journal helps by making the pattern visible before it becomes a crisis (<span class="link-private" title="Not public yet">What journaling is for</span>), and the structural fix is protecting a few hours for what matters and letting the rest wait ([Give the morning's best hours to deep work](/notes/give-the-morning-s-best-hours-to-deep-work)).

Michael Merzenich used microelectrodes to make fine maps of where a monkey's hand is represented in the brain — which neuron fires when which spot on the hand is touched.

Then he cut the sensory nerve and let it heal. The nerves grew back haphazardly, so touches mapped to the wrong places in the somatosensory cortex. After a few months, the brain had rewoven the map to match the hand again.

Later work confirmed this kind of remapping beyond the somatosensory cortex. The brain is plastic — and, per [The Shallows](/library/the-shallows-what-the-internet-is-doing-to-our-brains), that plasticity cuts both ways.

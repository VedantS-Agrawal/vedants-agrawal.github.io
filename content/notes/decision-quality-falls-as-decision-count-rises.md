The quality of my decisions deteriorates as I make more of them over a day. Decision fatigue looks like inaction, decision avoidance, procrastination, short-sighted and erratic behavior.

So the way to make good decisions is to make fewer of them. Systematize or eliminate most of the daily ones — how I wake up, what I do in the morning, how I work out, what I eat. Remove the rational mind's ability to negotiate. Let cues and triggers do the deciding (the house lights turn red at a set time).

Same idea in <span class="link-private" title="Not public yet">The Organized Mind</span>: the brain's decision-making network can't prioritize which decisions deserve its attention. Every decision taxes it, so restricting how many I face conserves it.

The other way to spend less on a decision: [Constraint relaxation](/notes/constraint-relaxation).

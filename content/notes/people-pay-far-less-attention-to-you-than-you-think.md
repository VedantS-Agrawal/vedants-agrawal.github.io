People really don't care what you're doing nearly as much as you think. They're paying attention to their own lives, their own realities, what's going on with them.

Sitting and observing a conversation without a goal: the words matter less than you'd think. Tonality carries much of the meaning in human conversation.

Psychologists call it the spotlight effect. In the classic study, students sent into a room wearing an embarrassing T-shirt guessed that about half the people there noticed; the actual figure was closer to a quarter. Everyone else is busy being the main character of their own day.

The self is loud mostly to itself: "Had the human self been installed with a mute button or off switch, the self would not be the curse to happiness that it often is." *— Mark Leary, quoted in Golden* The useful conclusion isn't that nobody cares, but that trying something and looking foolish costs far less than it feels.

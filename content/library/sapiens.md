- Homo sapiens came out on top of competition with other human species, though I don't think the book really analyzes *why* sapiens were more adept.
- What distinguishes humans: forming social groups oriented toward goals, using myth and culture to organize. Groups built on intimate bonds top out around 150 people. Beyond that, cultural myths, laws, rules, religion, and gods — structures that exist only in other people's minds — coordinate effort. See [Collective myths](/notes/collective-myths).
- Fire: light, and cooked food that cut the time and energy spent on digestion, supposedly freeing energy for a much larger brain. Interestingly, Neanderthals had bigger crania than we do.
- Some of Harari's arguments aren't entirely believable; others give a lot of food for thought.

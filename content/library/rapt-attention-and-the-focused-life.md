Personally, I found it quite great. It covered a lot of themes I'd already read, and a few sweeping cultural claims don't hold up — but there's much truth in it. It doesn't claim meditation is the answer; it argues that attention lies at the core of our existence.

The most interesting part: research on flow, and how early-childhood experiences set up the capacity for difficult work that makes later concentration and flow possible. Analyzing that across cultures could be fruitful.

A possible meditation target: two spheres of attention, top-down and bottom-up. We can set our objects of awareness — whether we attend to the positive or the negative is up to us.

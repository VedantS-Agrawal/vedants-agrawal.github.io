2008. The study behind my [Attention Restoration Theory](/notes/attention-restoration-theory) note.

The design is simple enough to remember. Participants took a walk either in an arboretum or along busy city streets, then repeated a backward digit-span test; directed attention improved after the nature walk and not after the urban one. A second experiment found a similar benefit from simply viewing pictures of nature rather than urban scenes.

The explanation is [Attention Restoration Theory](/notes/attention-restoration-theory): nature holds attention softly, letting the effortful, directed kind recover. The practical upshot is less glamorous than any productivity app — a walk between deep-work blocks ([Give the morning's best hours to deep work](/notes/give-the-morning-s-best-hours-to-deep-work)) — and it puts a mechanism under [What I attend to becomes my life](/notes/what-i-attend-to-becomes-my-life).

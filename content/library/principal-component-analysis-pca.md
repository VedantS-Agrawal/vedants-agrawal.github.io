Luis Serrano's channel explains machine learning with pictures before equations, and PCA suits the approach: a cloud of points, the direction along which they spread out most, then the next direction at right angles to it, and so on. Keep the first few directions, drop the rest, and most of the variance survives in far fewer dimensions.

The cost is interpretability — the new axes are blends of the original variables, and naming them is an act of judgment ([The Map is Not the Territory](/notes/the-map-is-not-the-territory)). The working notes are in [Principal Component Analysis](/notes/principal-component-analysis); the reason for learning it was [Cardiometabolic risk factor analysis](/notes/cardiometabolic-risk-factor-analysis), where a dozen correlated risk factors are begging to be summarized.

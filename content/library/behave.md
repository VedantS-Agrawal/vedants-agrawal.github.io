Learned about changes in hormonal expression.

Sapolsky's method is the useful part. Take one behavior and walk backward in time: the neurons that fired a second before, the hormones of the minutes and hours before, the environment of the preceding days, then adolescence, childhood, the womb, genes, culture, and finally evolution. Each chapter widens the lens by one step, and no single level gets to be *the* cause.

Hormones sit in the middle of that ladder, which makes them a good entry point. Sapolsky's recurring correction is that they rarely cause behavior outright; testosterone, for instance, tends to amplify tendencies already present rather than create new ones. It's the most patient possible answer to [Nature vs Nurture](/notes/nature-vs-nurture), and it lands close to <span class="link-private" title="Not public yet">Nature Via Nurture</span> — genes and environment as one tangled causal chain rather than rival teams. <span class="link-private" title="Not public yet">The Blank Slate</span> argues the same from the other direction.

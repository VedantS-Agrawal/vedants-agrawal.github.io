Really enjoyed it — political intrigue, character development, intertwined plot elements, and a fair amount of world-building drawn from the earlier films. The pacing was good, though I didn't expect a third act beyond his time as a mentor and the Games themselves.

The prequel's question is how Coriolanus Snow, the future tyrant of the series, became one. The tenth Hunger Games are losing their audience, Capitol students are assigned as mentors to make the spectacle watchable, and Snow draws District 12's Lucy Gray Baird. The film follows a young man choosing control over the people he says he loves.

Running underneath is an argument about human nature: the Head Gamemaker, Dr. Gaul, holds that people without order turn on each other, which is the whole justification for the Games. That's the debate <span class="link-private" title="Not public yet">The Blank Slate</span> picks apart, and the craft of steering people through their weaknesses is the subject of <span class="link-private" title="Not public yet">Weakness and manipulation in The Bonds of Interest</span>. The Games themselves are a [collective myth](/notes/collective-myths) with a body count.
